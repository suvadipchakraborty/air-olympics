What is this?

Air Olympics takes daily PM2.5 (fine particulate matter) readings for 100 cities worldwide and scores them like sporting events. Lower PM2.5 is better — it means fewer microscopic particles small enough to lodge in your lungs and bloodstream.

PM2.5 refers to airborne particles 2.5 micrometers or smaller — about 3% the width of a human hair. It's one of the most-watched indicators inside the broader Air Quality Index (AQI) because of how directly it's linked to respiratory and cardiovascular health.

The events

  • 100m Sprint — yesterday's raw reading. Pure current form.
  • Marathon — 7-day rolling average. Consistency over a sprint result.
  • Relay — change versus a month ago. Biggest improvement wins.
  • Weightlifting — the worst reading ever recorded for that city in this dataset. An anti-medal nobody wants.
  • Compare — pick any two cities and line up every metric to settle an argument.

How fresh is the data?

The board refreshes once a day. Each run pulls a new "Yesterday" reading per city and keeps the historical columns (day, month, and year lookbacks) alongside it, so trends and personal bests stay accurate as the days roll forward.

Data comes from the Open-Meteo Air Quality API, sampled daily and published as a rolling historical window. This project is satire wrapped around real numbers — the jokes are ours, the pollution is unfortunately everyone's.
